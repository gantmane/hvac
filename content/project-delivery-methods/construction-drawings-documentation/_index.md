Construction drawings represent the primary communication medium between design intent and field installation. For HVAC systems, the drawing set must convey three-dimensional spatial relationships, performance requirements, and installation specifications with precision sufficient to support accurate construction and regulatory compliance.

## Drawing Set Organization

### Mechanical Drawing Sequence

Standard practice organizes mechanical drawings according to CSI MasterFormat divisions:

**Drawing Type Sequence:**
- General mechanical notes and symbols (M-000 series)
- HVAC plans (M-100 series)
- Plumbing plans (M-200 series)
- Fire protection plans (M-300 series)
- Mechanical details (M-400 series)
- Mechanical schedules (M-500 series)
- Control diagrams (M-600 series)
- Sections and riser diagrams (M-700 series)

### Sheet Layout Standards

Each drawing sheet follows consistent organizational principles:

**Title Block Components:**
- Project identification and address
- Sheet title and number
- Drawing scale(s)
- North arrow orientation
- Revision history with dates
- Professional seal and signature
- Issue date and phase designation

**Drawing Area Organization:**
- Primary plan view occupies central position
- Enlarged details positioned peripherally
- Schedules placed in available space
- General notes located consistently across sheets
- Match lines coordinate with adjacent sheets

## HVAC Plan Drawings

### Plan View Content

Floor plans represent the horizontal projection of HVAC systems at specific cutting planes:

**Ductwork Representation:**
- Supply duct runs shown as single lines or double lines based on scale
- Return duct runs differentiated by line weight or pattern
- Exhaust duct runs identified by unique line type
- Duct sizes indicated at changes and regular intervals
- Fittings shown at direction changes
- Fire dampers, volume dampers, and access doors noted
- Flexible duct connections indicated

**Equipment Placement:**
- Air handling units with directional airflow arrows
- Rooftop units with curb dimensions
- Terminal units with serving zones identified
- Exhaust fans with discharge direction
- Equipment clearances for maintenance access
- Seismic restraint requirements noted

**Piping Systems:**
- Chilled water supply and return mains
- Heating hot water distribution
- Condensate drain routing
- Refrigerant piping for split systems
- Pipe sizes at all changes
- Isolation valves and balancing valves
- Pipe slope indicators for drainage
- Pipe support spacing requirements

**Terminal Devices:**
- Diffuser types and sizes
- Register and grille locations
- Linear slot diffuser runs
- Return air grille positions
- Thermostat locations
- Access panel requirements

### Plan View Scales

Drawing scale selection balances detail resolution with sheet coverage:

| Scale | Application | Detail Level |
|-------|------------|--------------|
| 1/16" = 1'-0" | Large buildings, overall layout | Minimal detail, major runs only |
| 1/8" = 1'-0" | Standard floor plans | Typical mechanical room detail |
| 1/4" = 1'-0" | Mechanical rooms, complex areas | Equipment detail, clearances |
| 1/2" = 1'-0" | Critical coordination zones | Full installation detail |

### Coordination Requirements

HVAC plans must coordinate with architectural, structural, electrical, plumbing, and fire protection disciplines:

**Ceiling Height Verification:**
- Finished ceiling elevations from architectural drawings
- Structural beam depths and locations
- Available plenum depth calculation
- Duct and pipe routing clearances
- Sprinkler head interference avoidance

**Structural Coordination:**
- Core penetrations through slabs and beams
- Roof curb locations and structural support
- Equipment pad locations and loading
- Hanger attachment points to structure
- Seismic bracing anchor points

**Electrical Coordination:**
- Power supply routing to equipment
- Disconnect switch locations
- Control wiring pathways
- Lighting fixture interference with ductwork
- Emergency power requirements

## Section Drawings

### Building Sections

Vertical sections cut through the building reveal vertical distribution systems and floor-to-floor relationships:

**Section Content:**
- Main supply and return duct risers
- Vertical pipe chases with pipe sizes
- Equipment room floor elevations
- Ceiling heights and plenum depths
- Structural members affecting routing
- Floor penetration details
- Vertical clearances to equipment

**Critical Dimensions:**
- Floor-to-floor heights
- Finished floor to underside of structure
- Bottom of duct to finished ceiling
- Equipment height and clearance requirements
- Roof penetration heights above roof surface

### Mechanical Room Sections

Sections through mechanical rooms provide installation guidance for complex equipment arrangements:

**Equipment Sections Show:**
- Duct connections to air handling units
- Pipe connections with vertical offsets
- Clearance dimensions for maintenance
- Access aisle widths
- Door swing clearances
- Overhead clearance for component removal
- Foundation and curb details
- Vibration isolation spring deflection

### Typical Section Details

Standardized sections document recurring installation conditions:

**Common Section Types:**
- Duct shaft vertical distribution
- Pipe chase with multiple systems
- Above-ceiling duct and pipe coordination
- Rooftop equipment curb and flashing
- Underfloor duct distribution
- Trenched piping in slab

- Turning vane installation in elbows
- Flexible duct connection methods

**Support Details:**
- Trapeze hanger assemblies
- Strap hanger spacing and sizing
- Anti-sway bracing methods

- Seismic restraint configurations
- Vibration isolation hanger selection

**Sealing Details:**
- Duct seam and joint sealing methods
- Penetration fire stopping
- Vapor barrier termination at equipment

**Pipe Connection Details:**

## Control Diagrams

### Sequence Diagrams

**Diagram Components:**
- Controlled devices (valves, dampers, fans)
- Sensors and measurement points
- Control logic decision points
- Setpoints and deadbands
- Interlocks and safety shutdowns
- Staging sequences for multiple units

**Diagram Types:**
- Psychrometric control sequence showing heating, cooling, economizing
- Boiler staging and rotation logic
- Chiller sequencing with tower coordination
- VAV box control with reheat
- Demand-controlled ventilation logic

### Point-to-Point Wiring Diagrams

Wiring diagrams detail electrical connections between control components:

**Wiring Diagram Content:**
- Controller input and output terminal designations
- Wire gauge and conductor count
- Voltage levels for power and control circuits
- Shield and ground connections
- Junction box locations
- Conduit routing requirements

### System Schematic Diagrams

Schematics represent system hydraulic or pneumatic relationships:

**Hydronic System Schematics:**
- Piping flow direction with arrows
- Pumps with flow and head
- Control valves with normally open/closed designation
- Coils with capacity ratings
- Isolation and balancing valve locations
- Expansion tank and air separator placement
- Temperature and pressure sensor locations

**Pneumatic Control Schematics:**
- Air compressor and receiver
- Main and branch air distribution
- Pressure reducing stations
- Thermostat and controller symbols
- Actuator spring ranges
- Tubing sizes and materials

### Symbol Libraries

Consistent graphical symbols represent HVAC components:

**Equipment Symbols:**
- Air handling unit: Rectangle with fan wheel symbol
- Exhaust fan: Circle with fan blade and directional arrow
- Boiler: Rectangle with wavy lines (fire symbol)
- Chiller: Rectangle with refrigeration cycle representation
- Pump: Circle with impeller blades
- Cooling tower: Rectangle with fill pattern

**Device Symbols:**
- Fire damper: FD in circle at duct
- Volume damper: VD with control line
- Diffuser: Square with diagonal lines
- Return grille: Square with horizontal lines
- Thermostat: T in circle
- Pressure gauge: PG in circle

**Valve Symbols:**
- Gate valve: Solid wedge
- Ball valve: Circle with diagonal line
- Globe valve: Circular with restriction
- Check valve: Arrow with barrier
- Butterfly valve: Line with disk
- Control valve: Valve symbol with actuator

### Dimensioning Practices

Accurate dimensioning enables field layout and verification:

**Dimension Requirements:**
- Equipment centerlines to structural grid
- Duct and pipe centerlines to walls
- Clearance dimensions around equipment
- Elevation dimensions to finished floor datum
- Penetration locations through structural members

**Dimension Chain Management:**

- Note which dimensions take precedence

## General Notes and Specifications

### Drawing Notes

General notes apply to all work of a particular type:

**Ductwork Notes:**
1. All ductwork shall comply with SMACNA HVAC Duct Construction Standards - Metal and Flexible.
2. Duct construction class as indicated on schedules; Class 2 minimum unless noted otherwise.
3. Seal all duct joints and seams to achieve Seal Class A per SMACNA.
4. Install fire dampers at all rated wall and floor penetrations per code requirements.
5. Provide turning vanes in all rectangular elbows.
6. Insulate all supply ductwork in unconditioned spaces with 2" fiberglass duct wrap, R-6 minimum.
7. Acoustically line ductwork as shown to achieve specified NC levels.

**Piping Notes:**
1. Provide full-port ball valves at all equipment connections for isolation.
2. Install strainers upstream of all control valves and specialty equipment.
3. Insulate all chilled water and heating hot water piping with fiberglass pipe insulation.
4. Pitch condensate drain piping 1/4" per foot minimum toward drain connection.
5. Provide unions at all equipment connections for service access.
6. Install pressure gauges and thermometer wells at equipment connections.
7. Flush and chemically clean all hydronic piping systems before operation.

**Installation Notes:**
1. Verify all dimensions in field before fabrication.
2. Coordinate work with other trades; notify engineer of conflicts.
3. Obtain engineer's written approval before deviating from drawings.
4. Provide vibration isolation for all rotating equipment per details.
5. Install seismic restraints per structural drawings and details.
6. Maintain minimum clearances for equipment access and maintenance.

### Specification Cross-References

Drawings reference technical specifications for detailed requirements:

**Specification Callouts:**
- Material specifications: "Ductwork per Section 23 31 00"
- Installation requirements: "Equipment installation per Section 23 05 13"
- Testing procedures: "Air balancing per Section 23 05 93"
- Performance criteria: "Sound levels per Section 23 01 50"

### Composite Overlay Drawings

Coordination drawings combine multiple disciplines to resolve conflicts:

**Overlay Content:**
- Structural beams and columns
- HVAC ductwork and piping
- Plumbing waste and vent
- Fire protection sprinkler mains
- Electrical conduit and cable tray
- Architectural reflected ceiling plan

**Conflict Resolution:**
- Identify spatial interferences
- Establish priority for routing
- Determine elevation adjustments
- Coordinate penetration locations
- Verify maintenance access preservation

### BIM Coordination Models

Building Information Modeling enables three-dimensional coordination:

**BIM Coordination Process:**
- Combine discipline-specific 3D models
- Execute automated clash detection
- Review clashes by priority and location
- Resolve conflicts through design adjustment
- Document resolutions in coordination reports
- Update construction documents to reflect coordination

## Shop Drawing Requirements

Construction drawings establish requirements for contractor-prepared shop drawings:

**Shop Drawing Submittal Requirements:**
- Equipment shop drawings with certified performance data
- Duct and pipe fabrication drawings for complex assemblies
- Equipment layout drawings showing clearances and connections
- Control system points list and sequences
- Coordination drawings for congested areas

**Review Criteria:**
- Compliance with design intent and specifications
- Dimensional accuracy and coordination
- Performance data verification
- Accessibility for maintenance
- Code compliance confirmation

## As-Built Documentation Requirements

Construction drawings form the basis for as-built records:

**As-Built Marking Requirements:**
- Red-line all field changes and modifications
- Document equipment substitutions and model numbers
- Record actual pipe and duct sizes if different from design
- Note control device locations and settings
- Identify concealed ductwork and piping routing
- Record penetration locations and firestopping details

**Record Drawing Deliverables:**
- Updated CAD files incorporating all as-built changes
- Equipment submittals with certified performance data
- Test and balance reports with final settings
- Control system programming and graphics
- Operations and maintenance manuals
- Warranty documentation

Comprehensive construction drawings serve as the contractual basis for HVAC system installation. The drawings must provide sufficient detail for accurate construction while maintaining clarity through organized presentation, standardized symbols, and proper annotation. Coordination between disciplines prevents conflicts and ensures systems integrate successfully within the building. Detailed schedules, sections, and details supplement plan views to communicate three-dimensional relationships and installation requirements. Proper documentation standards enable efficient construction, accurate as-built records, and long-term system maintenance.
