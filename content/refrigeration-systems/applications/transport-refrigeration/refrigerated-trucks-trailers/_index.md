Refrigerated trucks and trailers provide mobile cold chain preservation for temperature-sensitive cargo during transport operations. These systems maintain precise environmental control from loading dock to final delivery, ensuring product integrity across distribution networks.

## Trailer Refrigeration Units (TRUs)

### Mechanical Vapor Compression Systems

TRUs employ dedicated refrigeration systems mounted to the trailer front wall. The unit contains a complete vapor compression cycle with compressor, condenser, evaporator, expansion device, and controls integrated into a self-contained package. Engine-driven compressors connect via belt or direct drive to diesel engines rated 8-25 hp depending on trailer size and capacity requirements.

Capacity ranges from 15,000 to 60,000 BTU/hr for standard highway trailers. The refrigeration circuit uses R-452A or R-404A refrigerants in most contemporary installations, with newer units transitioning to lower-GWP alternatives. Condenser airflow rates of 3,000-6,000 CFM dissipate heat rejection loads, while evaporator fans circulate 2,500-5,000 CFM through the cargo space.

### Eutectic Plate Systems

Eutectic plates freeze during stationary charging periods and provide cooling capacity through phase change during transport. Salt solution eutectics freeze at specific temperatures (-20°F to +32°F) matched to cargo requirements. Plate arrays mount to trailer walls and ceiling, with total mass sized for trip duration and heat load magnitude.

These systems eliminate engine operation during transport, reducing fuel consumption and emissions. Charging requires 6-12 hours at dock facilities with electrical or mechanical refrigeration connections. Applications include urban delivery routes with predictable schedules and frequent dock access.

## Truck Body Construction

### Insulation Requirements

Trailer walls, floors, and ceilings require thermal resistance values of R-25 to R-35 to minimize heat transmission. Polyurethane foam provides continuous insulation with k-factors of 0.12-0.16 BTU·in/(hr·ft²·°F). Foam thickness ranges from 3 to 5 inches depending on operating temperature differential and structural requirements.

Floor insulation includes moisture barriers and thermal breaks to prevent heat ingress from road surface conduction. Aluminum or fiberglass-reinforced polymer exterior surfaces protect insulation from environmental exposure and mechanical damage. Interior surfaces use scuff-resistant materials suitable for sanitation protocols.

### Air Barrier Integrity

Trailer construction employs sealed joints and continuous vapor barriers to eliminate air infiltration. Door seals provide compression gaskets that maintain contact pressure across the opening perimeter. Infiltration rates should not exceed 0.05 CFM per square foot of door area when properly sealed.

Structural thermal bridges at aluminum extrusions and mounting points require thermal breaks. Uncontrolled thermal bridging can reduce effective R-value by 15-25% compared to insulation material specifications alone.

## Power Source Options

### Diesel Engine Drive

Diesel-powered TRUs operate independently of vehicle electrical systems, providing continuous cooling during transport and stationary periods. Fuel tanks hold 15-50 gallons for multi-day operation ranges. Engine speed modulates between idle (1,200-1,500 RPM) and full load (2,800-3,200 RPM) based on thermal demand.

Diesel systems deliver reliable capacity in extreme ambient conditions up to 115°F outdoor temperature. Maintenance intervals occur every 1,000-2,000 operating hours for oil changes, filter replacement, and system inspection. Diesel particulate filter (DPF) regeneration cycles occur automatically during operation.

### Electric Standby Systems

Electric standby connections provide facility power during dock operations, eliminating diesel engine runtime and associated emissions. Standby units draw 208-480V three-phase power at 15-30 kW depending on cooling capacity requirements. Automatic transfer switches engage electric operation when shore power connects, reverting to diesel drive when disconnected.

Electric operation reduces noise levels from 75-82 dBA (diesel) to 62-68 dBA, benefiting urban distribution centers with noise ordinances. Operating costs decrease $2-4 per hour compared to diesel fuel consumption. Many facilities mandate electric standby to comply with anti-idling regulations.

### All-Electric TRUs

Battery-electric and hybrid-electric TRUs eliminate diesel engines entirely. Battery packs rated 20-60 kWh provide 6-12 hours of cooling operation between charges. Regenerative charging from tractor alternators supplements battery capacity during highway transport.

These systems comply with zero-emission mandates in California CARB regulations and similar jurisdictions. Total cost of ownership analysis must account for battery replacement at 5-8 year intervals and charging infrastructure investments.

## Multi-Temperature Zone Systems

### Compartmentalized Cargo Space

Bulkheads with insulated partitions create separate temperature zones within single trailers. Two-zone configurations maintain frozen (-10°F to 0°F) and refrigerated (32°F to 38°F) compartments simultaneously. Three-zone systems add ambient (50°F to 60°F) sections for produce requiring elevated storage temperatures.

Each zone requires dedicated evaporator coils and airflow paths. Refrigerant distribution uses solenoid valves and electronic expansion devices to direct cooling capacity based on individual zone thermostats. Zone sizes typically range from 8 to 40 feet of trailer length.

### Control Complexity

Multi-zone systems employ microprocessor controllers managing refrigerant flow, airflow rates, and defrost cycles for each compartment. Temperature accuracy of ±1°F maintains product specifications across diverse cargo types. Control algorithms prevent thermal crosstalk between adjacent zones through insulation and airflow management.

## Temperature Monitoring Systems

### Data Logging Requirements

Electronic recorders document continuous temperature profiles throughout transport cycles. Sampling intervals of 1-5 minutes create audit trails for regulatory compliance and quality assurance programs. Memory capacity stores 30-90 days of data with battery backup during power interruptions.

USB and wireless download capabilities transfer records to fleet management systems. Alarm functions trigger at programmable temperature deviations, alerting drivers and dispatch personnel to system malfunctions or door seal failures.

### Sensor Placement

Return air temperature sensors provide the primary control input, measuring cargo heat load impact on refrigerated air. Supply air sensors verify evaporator performance and defrost cycle completion. Cargo probes in sensitive shipments monitor product core temperature independent of air temperature.

Sensor accuracy of ±0.5°F with annual calibration verification ensures measurement reliability for validation protocols.

## Regulatory Compliance

### DOT and CARB Standards

Department of Transportation regulations mandate equipment design and operation standards for food transport under the Food Safety Modernization Act (FSMA). Temperature control capabilities, monitoring systems, and maintenance documentation demonstrate compliance during inspections.

California Air Resources Board (CARB) regulations limit TRU engine emissions and require zero-emission technologies in specific applications. Tier 4 Final diesel engines or electric drive systems meet current emission standards. Compliance tracking through TRU identification numbers and operating hour documentation.

### ATP Certification

Agreement on the International Carriage of Perishable Foodstuffs (ATP) establishes thermal performance standards for international transport. Testing protocols measure heat transfer coefficients (k-values) and equipment cooling capacity. Certification plates document compliance with insulation class (IN, IR, FN, FR) and equipment type designations.
