Below-grade waterproofing protects foundation walls and slabs from groundwater intrusion. The system selection depends on hydrostatic pressure conditions, soil characteristics, and interior space usage requirements.

## Damp Proofing vs Waterproofing

**Damp proofing** resists moisture vapor and minor dampness but cannot withstand hydrostatic pressure. Applied as spray-on or rolled coatings (bituminous emulsions, polymer-modified cementitious materials), damp proofing is suitable only for sites with good drainage where the water table remains below the foundation depth year-round.

**Waterproofing** provides a continuous, impermeable barrier that resists hydrostatic pressure from groundwater. Required when:
- Water table may rise above foundation base
- Interior spaces will be occupied or climate-controlled
- Soil has poor drainage characteristics (clay, silt)
- Foundation extends more than 3 feet below grade
- Site lacks adequate positive drainage away from building

## Hydrostatic Pressure Conditions

Hydrostatic pressure develops when groundwater accumulates against foundation walls without drainage relief.

**Pressure calculation:**
P = γw × h

Where:
- P = hydrostatic pressure (psf)
- γw = unit weight of water (62.4 lb/ft³)
- h = height of water column above measurement point (ft)

Example: 8-foot water column = 62.4 × 8 = 499 psf (3.47 psi)

**Design considerations:**
- Water table elevation during maximum seasonal rise
- Perched water conditions from low-permeability soil layers
- Surface water infiltration patterns
- Drainage system capacity and reliability
- Foundation wall structural capacity to resist lateral loads

High hydrostatic pressure (>5 feet of head) requires robust waterproofing systems with redundancy, drainage relief, and structural foundation design to resist bending moments.

## Waterproof Membrane Systems

### Sheet Membranes

**Self-adhered rubberized asphalt:**
- Composition: Rubberized asphalt compound on polyethylene carrier with release liner
- Thickness: 40-60 mils typical
- Application: Adheres directly to primed concrete/masonry
- Advantages: Self-sealing around penetrations, cold-applied, immediate protection
- Limitations: Surface prep critical, temperature-sensitive installation, moderate cost
- Products: W.R. Grace Bituthene 4000, Carlisle CCW-705

**Self-adhered HDPE:**
- Composition: High-density polyethylene sheet with aggressive adhesive
- Thickness: 30-45 mils HDPE + adhesive layer
- Application: Applied to primed surface, mechanically fastened at top
- Advantages: Excellent puncture resistance, dimensional stability, UV-resistant during construction
- Limitations: Requires proper surface moisture content, limited self-sealing
- Products: Henry Blueskin WP200, W.R. Grace Preprufe Plus

**Bentonite sheets:**
- Composition: Sodium bentonite clay between geotextile and HDPE layers
- Thickness: Bentonite layer 15-20 lb/100 ft² (compressed to ~4mm)
- Application: Mechanically fastened or adhered panels with overlapped seams
- Advantages: Self-healing, tolerates damp substrates, low installed cost
- Limitations: Requires hydration and confinement pressure, not suitable for flowing water, contaminated water reduces swelling
- Products: Voltex DS, Cetco Hydrotite

**EPDM/TPO sheets:**
- Composition: Ethylene propylene diene monomer or thermoplastic polyolefin
- Thickness: 45-60 mils typical for waterproofing
- Application: Mechanically fastened or adhered with heat-welded seams
- Advantages: Long-term durability, chemical resistance, factory-controlled seams
- Limitations: Seaming requires skilled labor, vulnerable to puncture during backfill
- Products: Firestone RubberGard, Carlisle Sure-Weld TPO

**PVC sheets:**
- Composition: Polyvinyl chloride with plasticizers
- Thickness: 30-60 mils
- Application: Mechanically fastened with heat-welded or solvent-welded seams
- Advantages: Excellent chemical resistance, heat-weldable seams, root-resistant
- Products: Sika Sarnafil, Soprema PVC

### Fluid-Applied Membranes

**Polymer-modified asphalt:**
- Composition: Asphalt emulsion with polymer modifiers (SBR, acrylic)
- Application: Spray, trowel, or squeegee in multiple coats
- Dry film thickness: 60-120 mils total (2-3 coats)
- Advantages: Seamless application, conforms to complex geometries, self-terminating at penetrations
- Limitations: Cure time required before backfill, thickness control critical, weather-dependent application
- Products: Tremco TREMproof 250GC, W.R. Grace Bituthene Liquid Membrane

**Polyurethane coatings:**
- Composition: Single or two-component polyurethane
- Application: Spray or roller applied
- Dry film thickness: 40-80 mils (multiple coats)
- Advantages: Excellent elongation (>300%), cold-weather application, fast cure
- Limitations: Moisture-sensitive during cure, requires reinforcing fabric at transitions, higher cost
- Products: BASF MasterSeal NP 150, Tremco Watchdog NP-1

**Cementitious crystalline:**
- Composition: Portland cement, silica sand, active chemicals
- Application: Brush, spray, or trowel applied to dampened substrate
- Thickness: 1/16 to 1/8 inch typical
- Advantages: Bonds to concrete, self-healing through continued crystalline growth, applicable to negative-side applications
- Limitations: Requires moisture for activation, rigid (no crack-bridging), limited to concrete substrates
- Products: Xypex Concentrate, Kryton Krystol T1

**Spray-applied polyurea/polyurethane:**
- Composition: Fast-reacting two-component spray system
- Application: Plural-component spray equipment
- Dry film thickness: 60-125 mils in single pass
- Advantages: Rapid application, monolithic membrane, immediate protection, excellent elongation
- Limitations: Specialized equipment required, skilled applicators necessary, surface prep critical
- Products: VersaFlex, SWD Urethane, Polyguard RapidHyde

## Sheet vs Fluid-Applied Comparison

| Characteristic | Sheet Membranes | Fluid-Applied Membranes |
|---------------|-----------------|------------------------|
| Thickness uniformity | Consistent factory thickness | Variable, depends on application |
| Seams/joints | Require overlaps and sealing | Seamless, monolithic |
| Complex geometry | Difficult, requires cutting/fitting | Excellent conformability |
| Application speed | Moderate to fast | Varies by type |
| QC verification | Visual inspection | Thickness gauges required |
| Penetration detailing | Requires careful cutting and sealing | Self-terminating |
| Substrate moisture | Critical for adhesion | More tolerant (most types) |
| Cost (material) | Moderate to high | Moderate to high |
| Labor skill required | Moderate | High for spray systems |
| Crack-bridging | Limited by sheet elongation | Excellent (urethanes, polyureas) |

## Protection Boards

Protection boards shield waterproofing membranes from damage during backfill operations and throughout building life.

**High-density polyethylene (HDPE) boards:**
- Thickness: 1/4 to 1/2 inch
- Compressive strength: 3,000-5,000 psi
- Application: Mechanically fastened to wall or adhered to membrane
- Advantages: High impact resistance, dimensionally stable, lightweight
- Products: Cetco Watchdog Protecto Board, Tremco Protecto Board

**Expanded polystyrene (EPS) boards:**
- Thickness: 1 to 2 inches
- Compressive strength: 10-60 psi (varies by density)
- Application: Adhered or mechanically fastened
- Advantages: Combined insulation and protection, cost-effective
- Limitations: Lower impact resistance than HDPE
- Products: Standard EPS insulation boards

**Extruded polystyrene (XPS) boards:**
- Thickness: 1 to 2 inches
- Compressive strength: 15-60 psi
- Application: Adhered to wall or membrane
- Advantages: Higher compressive strength than EPS, moisture-resistant
- Products: Owens Corning Foamular, Dow Styrofoam

**Asphaltic fiberboard:**
- Thickness: 1/2 to 3/4 inch
- Density: 18-22 lb/ft³
- Application: Mechanically fastened
- Advantages: Conformable, traditional material
- Limitations: Absorbs moisture, degrades over time, largely obsolete

**Geocomposite drainage boards:**
- Composition: Drainage core (dimpled plastic, geotextile fabric) bonded to filter fabric
- Thickness: 1/4 to 1/2 inch core
- Function: Combined protection and drainage
- Advantages: Single-component system, high drainage capacity
- Products: Tremco Watchdog Drain Sheet, Cetco Voltex Drain

## Termination Details

Proper terminations prevent water infiltration at system boundaries and transitions.

**Top-of-wall termination:**
- Extend membrane 6-12 inches above final grade
- Mechanical termination bar anchored to wall
- Sealant bead between membrane and bar
- Flashing or cant strip to protect exposed edge
- Metal flashing extended over termination and integrated with wall drainage plane

**Footing transition:**
- Continue membrane onto footing top surface minimum 12 inches
- Seal to footing with compatible mastic or sealant
- Bentonite systems require confinement at footing (mud slab or membrane overlap)

**Penetrations (pipes, conduits):**
- Sheet systems: Cut membrane, seal with mastic, apply boot or patch
- Fluid-applied: Build up thickness around penetration, self-terminating
- Waterstops at pipe penetrations through structural wall
- Maintain minimum 6-inch clearance between penetrations for sealing

**Cold joints in concrete:**
- Waterstops installed during concrete placement
- Membrane adhered over waterstop
- Additional sealant or mastic at joint
- Crystalline systems applied to joint with focus treatment

**Inside/outside corners:**
- Reinforce with additional membrane strip or fabric
- Radius corners where possible (min 1-inch radius)
- Fluid-applied systems: Additional coat at corners
- Sheet systems: Prefabricated corner pieces recommended

## Quality Control

**Pre-application inspection:**
- Substrate surface profile (CSP 1-2 for most systems)
- Substrate moisture content (<5% for adhesion-dependent systems)
- Temperature and weather conditions within manufacturer specifications
- Curing time verification for concrete (typically 14-28 days)
- Surface cleanliness (no oil, dirt, laitance, form release agents)

**During application:**
- Thickness verification (wet film gauges for fluid-applied)
- Overlap dimensions (6-inch minimum typical for sheets)
- Adhesion testing (random pull tests for self-adhered systems)
- Seam integrity (visual inspection, test cuts)
- Coverage rates and material consumption tracking
- Documentation with photographs at all penetrations and transitions

**Post-application inspection:**
- Holiday detection (electrical conductance testing for conductive membranes)
- Flood testing (when feasible before backfill)
- Seam probe testing
- Dry film thickness verification (destructive core samples for fluid-applied)
- Protection board installation before backfill authorization

**Flood testing protocol:**
- Applicable for horizontal surfaces and enclosed sections
- Maintain water level minimum 2 inches above highest point for 24-72 hours
- Inspect interior surfaces for moisture
- Mark and repair any leaks, retest repaired areas

**Backfill requirements:**
- Controlled backfill in 12-inch lifts maximum
- Free of rocks, debris, and sharp objects
- Compaction to specified density without damaging protection board
- Adequate foundation drainage system operational before final backfill

## System Selection Criteria

**For low hydrostatic pressure (<3 ft head):**
- Self-adhered rubberized asphalt with drainage board
- Bentonite sheets (if groundwater clean)
- Polymer-modified asphalt fluid-applied

**For high hydrostatic pressure (>8 ft head):**
- Self-adhered HDPE with redundant drainage
- Spray-applied polyurea with reinforcement
- Bentonite with secondary drainage plane
- Consider double membrane systems for critical applications

**For occupied below-grade spaces:**
- Premium sheet systems (EPDM, HDPE)
- Fluid polyurethane with high elongation
- Include vapor barrier on interior if required
- Dehumidification system for interior climate control

**For difficult substrates:**
- Fluid-applied systems conform to irregular surfaces
- Cementitious crystalline for damp concrete
- Mechanically fastened systems for low-strength masonry
