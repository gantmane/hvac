The bidding phase represents the critical transition from design to construction, requiring thorough preparation of bid documents, rigorous contractor evaluation, and systematic management of the competitive selection process for HVAC systems.

## Bid Document Preparation

Complete bid packages include drawings, specifications, general conditions, supplementary conditions, and contract forms. The HVAC engineer verifies that mechanical drawings accurately reflect design intent with correct equipment schedules, duct and pipe sizing, control sequences, and demolition requirements. Cross-reference all equipment tags between drawings and Division 23 specifications to eliminate conflicts.

Specification review confirms that performance requirements, submittal procedures, testing protocols, and closeout documents are clearly stated. Division 01 requirements for submittals, substitutions, project meetings, and quality control integrate with mechanical-specific requirements in Division 23.

The AIA A701 Instructions to Bidders establishes bidding procedures, bid form requirements, and evaluation criteria. Supplementary instructions address HVAC-specific requirements including equipment staging areas, crane access, rigging limitations, vibration-sensitive areas, and operational constraints in occupied facilities.

## Pre-Bid Meetings

Pre-bid meetings allow contractors to inspect existing conditions, review coordination requirements, and clarify scope ambiguities. For renovation projects, contractors examine existing mechanical systems, available utility capacities, demolition sequences, and tie-in logistics. Site visits reveal concealed conditions, access restrictions, laydown areas, and temporary utility requirements that impact HVAC installation costs.

Document all questions raised during pre-bid meetings with written responses distributed as formal addenda. Address refrigerant line lengths, equipment rigging paths, electrical service adequacy, structural support requirements, and control system integration scope. Clarifications become part of the contract documents and supersede conflicting information in original bid sets.

## Addenda Management

Addenda issued during the bidding period modify drawings, specifications, or contract terms. Number addenda sequentially and require contractors to acknowledge receipt of all addenda with bid submissions. HVAC addenda typically address equipment substitutions, revised performance specifications, duct routing modifications, or clarifications of control sequences.

Issue addenda sufficiently before bid opening to allow contractors adequate time for pricing adjustments. Most jurisdictions require addenda issued within 72-96 hours of bid opening to trigger automatic bid date extensions. Track addendum distribution to all plan holders and confirm receipt before accepting bids.

Coordinate addenda with other disciplines to prevent cascading changes. A structural addendum affecting ceiling heights may require mechanical duct routing revisions. An electrical addendum changing voltage characteristics necessitates motor and starter specification updates.

## Contractor Prequalification

Prequalification establishes minimum contractor qualifications before bid submission. Evaluate mechanical contractor experience with similar HVAC system types, project scale, and building occupancy classifications. Review completed projects demonstrating proficiency with central chilled water plants, complex DDC systems, mission-critical environments, or healthcare ventilation requirements.

Assess financial capacity through bonding limits, credit references, and past project payment histories. Contractors must demonstrate sufficient bonding capacity for the project contract value plus other ongoing work commitments. Review safety records including OSHA incident rates, Experience Modification Rates (EMR), and safety program documentation.

Verify licensing requirements for mechanical contracting, sheet metal fabrication, refrigeration work, and medical gas installation where applicable. Confirm union affiliations align with project labor requirements. Evaluate subcontractor qualification procedures for specialty work including test and balance, controls programming, and equipment commissioning.

## Bid Evaluation

Systematic bid evaluation compares pricing, equipment compliance, alternates, and qualifications. Construct bid tabulation spreadsheets listing base bid amounts, alternate prices, unit costs, allowances, and completion schedules from all bidders. Identify outlier bids requiring additional scrutiny for scope gaps or errors.

Review submitted equipment data cuts for compliance with specified performance requirements. Verify proposed chillers meet efficiency minimums, boilers achieve required turndown ratios, air handlers provide specified fan performance, and controls systems include all specified points and sequences. Non-compliant equipment submissions may render bids non-responsive.

Evaluate proposed substitutions against specifications. AIA A701 typically restricts substitutions during bidding to those specifically allowed by the specifications. Post-bid substitutions require demonstration of equivalence without cost or schedule impacts. HVAC equipment substitutions must maintain specified capacities, efficiencies, sound levels, and dimensional constraints.

Compare unit prices for add/deduct quantities, change order work, and time-and-material rates. These values become critical during construction for pricing scope changes. Abnormally high unit prices may indicate contractor risk loading or inadequate competition.

## Value Engineering

Value engineering proposals during bidding offer alternative designs or products reducing first cost while maintaining performance requirements. Contractors may propose alternative chiller types, different air distribution strategies, or alternative control system platforms. The engineer evaluates proposals for performance equivalence, lifecycle cost impacts, operational complexity, and maintainability.

Rigorous analysis examines energy consumption changes, maintenance requirement differences, equipment life expectancy, and replacement cost implications. A lower-efficiency chiller reducing first cost by $50,000 may increase annual energy costs by $15,000, creating negative lifecycle value. Document all value engineering decisions with cost-benefit calculations.

Reject value engineering proposals compromising system reliability, reducing redundancy, eliminating required code compliance features, or creating long-term operational problems. Maintain design intent for critical parameters including ventilation rates, filtration levels, temperature control accuracy, and humidity stability in sensitive spaces.

## Bid Opening and Award Recommendation

Public bid openings announce bid amounts from all contractors. Record base bids, alternates, unit prices, and completion times. Distribute bid tabulations to the owner within 24-48 hours with preliminary compliance review results.

The engineer's recommendation considers bid amount, contractor qualifications, equipment compliance, financial capacity, and project understanding. The lowest responsible bidder meeting specifications typically receives award recommendation. Non-responsive bids containing material specification deviations, incomplete documentation, or unacknowledged addenda may be rejected.

Document the recommendation with detailed analysis of equipment compliance, qualifications review, reference checks, and cost reasonableness assessment. Address any concerns regarding abnormally low bids potentially indicating scope misunderstanding or financial distress. The owner makes the final award decision based on the engineer's recommendation and project-specific considerations.

## Post-Award Activities

Following contract award, conduct pre-construction meetings establishing communication protocols, submittal procedures, meeting schedules, and construction phasing requirements. Review the contractor's preliminary schedule for equipment procurement lead times, critical path activities, and coordination milestones. Clarify gray areas in contract documents before construction mobilization.

Establish submittal registers tracking required product data, shop drawings, performance calculations, and commissioning documentation. Define review timeframes, resubmittal procedures, and approval authorities. Confirm testing requirements, startup procedures, training obligations, and warranty documentation protocols before construction commences.
